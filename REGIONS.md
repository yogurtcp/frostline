# Endless areas

The playlist is `game-config.json` → `endless.regions`. Areas join without ending the run. Existing pursuers stay alive across boundaries. Arcade layouts and town cadence are unchanged.

The first **450 metres use the original gentle opening**. Then the normal sequence is **town → quiet → tree corridors → lake → quiet → cemetery → dense forest → regular forest → wild patch → quiet**, repeating. Wide crowd encounters are inserted randomly farther downhill without skipping the next normal area.

| Config kind | Base length | Core challenge | Gates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `crowd` | 320–400 m | Rush Hour Slope: a broad, busy field with skiers across every lane, hunting Yetis and tree banks along both edges. Appears randomly later. | Normal slalom |
| `quiet` | 180–250 m | Snowy Trail: continues the actual town-exit snow road, with scattered nearby trees, a few walkers, slow skiers, a bench/lamp and snowman. Sparse woods continue off the path. | None |
| `trees` | 900–1,200 m | Existing long bends, forks, alternating gates and dense tree banks. | Weaving gates |
| `lake` | 240–330 m | Huge clear ice: set a heading, coast, then regain control on the quiet snow afterward. | None |
| `town` | 900–1,200 m | Actual Arcade-style village, narrowing street, pedestrians and back route. | Entry/exit only |
| `cemetery` | 650–850 m | One downhill aisle with four→five moderate diagonal crossings and straight downhill connectors. Close rails/graves keep it narrow; occasional masonry shoulders guide the same route. Blue lurkers visibly peek from graves before one leap; more green/brown walkers shuffle and eat on contact. Y motion stays at 40%. | Entry/exit only |
| `denseForest` | 110–160 m | A short thick stand of trees with a narrow winding passage. Concentrate on threading through or jumping. | None |
| `scramble` | 70–110 m | Wild Patch: short randomized scatter of several tree species, rocks, stumps, bushes, sleds, small jumps and skier/boarder traffic. Find a gap or jump; no reserved centre corridor. | None |
| `forest` | 300–450 m | Ordinary sparse forest, slalom, traffic, smaller random lakes and occasional hills/rainbows. | Normal slalom |

`regions.minimumWidthMetres` is **600**. Cemeteries, towns, tree corridors, dense forest, Wild Patch, crowd fields and clear lakes keep their theme across at least that full width, scaled with entry pace. Cemetery flanks contain additional fenced burial plots, monuments, mausoleums and sparse zombie ambushers; town flanks contain irregular chalet blocks using the native row spacing. Forest and Wild Patch flanks retain their own obstacle types. Broad entry/exit tree wings or graveyard fences make a quick detour around the section harder, while the intended openings stay clear. These are physical obstacles, not invisible boundaries. Low fences and forest banks are jumpable; cemetery stone shoulders require their openings. Ordinary sparse woods remain beyond the distant edges. Quiet trails and ordinary open forest keep their relief layouts.

Lengths scale with entry pace. They stay fixed for a visit, as does that visit's difficulty. Repeated crowds increase population, cemeteries narrow from 7.4 toward 5.4 base metres, with selected necks tightening from 5.2 toward 3.8 metres and more zombies, dense forests narrow their clear passage and increase tree density 1→2×, and regular forest density rises. Town and corridor progression retain their existing settings. Snowy trails and clear lakes remain relief; trail traffic stays light rather than increasing with visits.

`regions.order` can reorder or repeat any kind. `denseForestDensity` [1,2] sets first/final-visit tree density for Close Pines; the original first-visit geometry stays intact. `scrambleDensity` [1,1.35] separately controls Wild Patch scatter. Both freeze difficulty within a section and grow between visits. Wild Patch and Close Pines are separate kinds with independent visit counts. After a town, Snowy Trail extends that town’s existing approach road along the skier’s approach, then gently rejoins the course centre. Its width stays at Arcade’s 64 units, including later visits; it does not narrow with the town. Only its immediate path strip is cleared, so leaving it does not expose a large empty region. `regions.lengthMetres` controls the new area lengths; `endless.encounters.lengthMetres` still controls towns and long tree corridors. `regions.gapMetres` can add ordinary connector terrain. `regions.enabled: false` restores the preceding town/tree schedule.

`regions.crowd` controls random crowds: first eligible after 1,200 m, 55% chance at a safe transition, at least 1,600 m after the previous crowd ends, and forced at the next eligible transition after 4,200 m. Eligible transitions follow quiet/open forest, so crowds do not interrupt ice exits. Set chance to 0 to disable random crowds. Width is the larger of `crowd.widthMetres` (240) and `minimumWidthMetres` (600), scaled with entry pace; the slope is centred on the approach, populated out to both edges and bounded by dense tree banks. The configured 180→280 people are the total field population, capped at 600; increasing themed width no longer multiplies that count. The broad field and side banks remain, with more room between skiers. `regions.lengthMetres.crowd` controls length. The old `beginner`/`beginnerPeople` settings remain accepted as legacy explicit-layout options. `quietTreesPer100Metres` is 8 and `forestTreesPer100Metres` is [12,26]. `cemeteryOpeningMetres` is [5.4,7.4] in late/first order, matching the existing opening configuration convention. `zombies.count` is [18,36] in the central cemetery, with sparse additional guards in streamed outer plots (zero disables both), `speedKmh` is [7,11], and `noticeMetres` is 65. Ambush range/reach use the common pace scaling and the cemetery Y multiplier applies to leap motion. Blue ambushers leap once and then remain stationary; spent bodies use normal scenery cleanup rather than persisting as distant pursuers. Separate `wandererCount: [24,48]` and `wandererSpeedKmh: [2,3.5]` add slow green/brown shamblers, with four upright native pixel designs. They wander near home until noticing the player, then follow the cemetery route slowly. Setting the wanderer count to zero disables this role independently. Living predators do not treat undead as edible prey.

There are no new predator placements in quiet areas, clear lakes, dense forests, Wild Patches, cemeteries or towns; authored zombies are the cemetery's threat. Crowds can add a Yeti when the nearby hunter budget allows. Existing enemies can follow through. Monsters stay off ice. A blue ambush knocks down an upright skier rather than instantly eating them. The same attacker cannot immediately eat its own victim; another descending/spent zombie or a Yeti can catch the fallen skier. Player jumps, swords and genuine full-speed rams still work. Tombstones and iron fences can be jumped; fences have real openings and block grounded skiers and creatures. Mausoleum roofs retain building collisions. Wanderers use the upright hiker, gravedigger, skeleton and shrouded designs; blue ambushers use a separate hunched silhouette. Static cemetery art is cached. Hidden and spent ambushers do not run roaming/pursuit searches; only their live leap moves. Wanderers retain slow roaming and pursuit.

Player-initiated rams require actual swept body contact, near-full speed for the current steering angle, and genuine approach/closing motion above `physics.minimumCollisionKmh` (8). The shared `wildlife.ramSpeedFraction` is .85 of attainable sliding/traverse speed for the current heading/mode; fixed species gates no longer override it. Perfect centre aim is not required, and collision checks keep the player's original impact motion even if another collision has just knocked them down in the same frame. A matched-speed touch or a pursuer catching the player from behind does not kill the enemy. Previously fallen players do not acquire a ram attack. Sleeping/waking/waiting monsters remain collidable, including fast glancing contacts between samples. Slower solid hits on those bodies crash the skier instead of only reducing speed. Jumps still clear them. Old species speed and bear/Yeti aiming-cone settings remain accepted for config compatibility.

No saved record version was reset for this expansion. Wallet, settings and the current Endless best stay intact.

## Possible additions

These are suggestions, not active area types:

- **Cemetery bell gates:** a shortcut through a narrow opening rings a bell and wakes nearby graves; a longer open route avoids the alarm. Suggested mechanic only, not active.
- **Wind pass:** gentle alternating gusts push the skier sideways through otherwise open terrain; anticipation matters more than obstacle density.
- **Jump meadow:** optional chains of hills offer faster routes, while occasional gate placements reward staying grounded and choosing a line.
- **Frozen river:** narrow snow crossings and short slippery ice shortcuts create a choice between precise steering and committing to an angle.

Towns each have one cocoa shop, claimed once at its front window for one shield and a cozy card dismissed by tap/key. The simulation and pursuers pause during the card, then resume at the same position. Normal shields fill the current run inventory; overflow/free-play gifts enter a saved cocoa-shield reserve. Predator deaths also show funny aftermath cards dismissed by tap/key, with attacker-matched Yeti art, before returning to the summit.

All killable enemies share `resolveEnemyContact`: successful rams kill the creature **and crash the skier**, using normal stopped-motion/fall/recovery behavior with no new immunity or equipment consumption.

Wide flank scenery streams in nearby world chunks, unloads beyond the surrounding window and regenerates with stable geometry when revisited. Revealing/leaping zombies detach from chunk cleanup, then become stationary and age out as scenery; their spawn IDs prevent duplicate live guards on re-entry. Hunters pursue directly when targets use the outer terrain instead of returning to a central route. Distant horizontal scenery is culled before render-layer sorting. Arcade towns, other region roads/gates and the gentle opening retain their settings.

Cemeteries use `regions.cemetery`: Y movement is 40% of normal, blending over 25 base metres at the boundaries; X movement and event timers stay responsive. This applies locally to the skier, traffic, pedestrians, animals, pursuers and moving hazards, including player jumps. The displayed speed uses actual movement. Four→five diagonal crossings span up to 90→120 base metres across; the first and last cover half that span. Diagonal grade is computed from the local Y factor and one keyboard steering step (about 35° from horizontal at the defaults), so one steering press follows the diagonal. Downhill stems connect them, with no horizontal return leg. Older/larger crossing settings are bounded to fit the available section length. Entry/exit have short vertical stems for the usual gates. Ordinary stage length/distance still measures downhill span, so following every switchback covers more ground. Garden fences follow the crossings and preserve the full clear lane. Hunters and on-way prey checks use distance along these paths.

`regions.zombies.ambush` controls the 24 m trigger radius, 0.6 s emergence tell, 0.65 s base leap, 50 m nominal reach before Y projection and 60-world-unit visual arc. Range/reach use the existing pace scale. The existing 7→11 `speedKmh` values now make later leaps quicker rather than producing continuous walking pursuit. `noticeMetres` controls wanderer awareness; the separate ambush radius controls the blue zombie reveal. Each leap aims once at the predicted player position, so changing direction can dodge it. Hidden bodies cannot collide; descending leaps can bump, while landed zombies stay put. No recovery immunity is added against other enemies.

`cemetery.chokeWidthMetres: [3.8,5.2]` uses late/first ordering, like `cemeteryOpeningMetres`. Wider corner landings separate the tight stretches. Occasional masonry shoulders span the themed field with one broad opening around the same aisle at a bend; jumping or shield protection cannot phase through them. Rails and nearby crypts crowd the intended aisle, and the route remains physically clear for the skier. A wanderer is lethal to a grounded skier, including while recovering; jumping, swords and physical full-speed rams use the common enemy rules. Blue ambushers retain their nonlethal first bump and one-shot landing behavior. Their head, shoulders and arms remain visible while hidden, and they rise smoothly from that partial pose. Burial plots use their actual oriented corners, rather than path-projected patches that resembled extra branches.
