# Frostline configuration

Edit `game-config.json`. For local tuning, run `python3 serve.py` in this folder and open http://127.0.0.1:8765/. Every reload reads the JSON directly; no rebuild is needed for config edits. The hosted game also reads it on refresh.

Opening `index.html` through `file://` uses its embedded snapshot: browsers restrict reading adjacent local files. To update that standalone snapshot, run `python3 build.py`, then reload. Localhost and file URLs have separate browser saves (wallet and records); switching addresses does not transfer them. Code/schema edits still require a build.

Zero Yeti speed is allowed and means stationary. A nonzero count does not automatically assign a chase speed.

The file contains tuning data; collision algorithms, AI logic, pixel glyph shapes and renderer drawing instructions remain in JavaScript. Structural changes such as inventing a new object type still require code.

## Units and sections

World coordinates increase rightward and downhill. `world.unitsPerMetre` defaults to 10. Speeds are world units per second unless the property explicitly says `Kmh`. Durations are seconds. The HUD converts speed using `physics.hudKmhPerSpeed`. Ranges are `[minimum, maximum]`; probabilities are 0–1.

| Section | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| `endless` | Separate continuous Mixed Slalom: gradual pressure, rolling terrain generation, gates/gear, lake sizes, traffic and capped Yeti encounters |
| `arcade` | 27 compact stage rows plus central knob tables, connected-stage/town flow, stage defaults/overrides, terrain, traffic, wolf-guard penalties and run-result duration |
| `courses` | Names, fees, lengths in metres, pace, gate spacing, obstacle density and type |
| `race` | Gate widths, required success fraction, point rewards, finish bonuses, choice rows and scenery placement |

| `physics` | Acceleration, top speed, turning, walking, braking, ice, road slowdown, collisions and crash recovery |

## Useful examples

- Arcade stage editor: `arcade.stages` maps names to knob tuples (`"Name": [obstacles, chase, miss, speed, ice]`, 1–4 each) in play order, with arbitrary names. Details resolve from `arcade.stageDefaults` plus per-name `arcade.stageOverrides` (gate count/swords, yeti color, two rarity-tier rates, overrides). Gate ice share comes from the ice knob. See `ARCADE.md` for a copyable pair and all keys.
- Arcade equipment: each stage’s `gates.swordGateCount` selects the number of sword gates. `arcade.gear.shieldsPerStage` controls shield yield, `swordGatesPerSword` controls sword charge, and `maxShields` / `maxSwords` cap inventory.
- Arcade item density: `arcade.itemTiers` assigns each of the 64 objects to `common`, `slightlyRare` or `veryRare`; the obstacle knob sets the `common` per-100m rate, each stage's `tierRates` sets the other two, and `itemRates` overrides single objects (0 disables; omission uses the tier/knob rate). `skier`, `fastSkier`, `boarder` and `overtakingSkier` are separate, and `endTrafficMultiplier` increases their density toward the finish. Other object densities stay constant. Safe gate openings and landing clearances can reduce actual placement.
- Make regular slalom timing more forgiving: increase `timing.targets.slalom.goldSeconds` and `parSeconds`, keeping gold below par.
- Reduce all speed prizes: lower `timing.maxBonus` (60 by default).
- Slow recovery: `physics.minimumCollisionKmh` (8) disables obstacle impacts only when BOTH you and the object are at or below that HUD speed in absolute terms. Creeping into a stationary dog is harmless, but a fast skier hitting a stopped player still crashes, and so does cruising into a same-speed boarder. Objects touched gently stay harmless until you fully clear them, even if you accelerate. Chasers (wolves, patrol) keep the old closing-speed rule, so outrunning them still counts as escaping.
- Bear/yeti impacts: `wildlife.bearKnockoutKmh` and `yetiKnockoutKmh` are 65/85; `bearMaxImpactAngleDegrees` and `yetiMaxImpactAngleDegrees` are 45/10. Speed alone cannot override the angle limit. Successful impacts kill the animal; it fades over `predatorFadeSeconds`.
- Make wolves easier to hit: lower `wildlife.wolfKillKmh` (30 by default; the actual impact must be faster than the threshold).
- Less harassment: lower `skiers.fastAimWeight`, increase `fastSpawnSeconds`, or lower `ambientFastChance` and `courseFastChance`.
- More houses: increase `village.houseCount`; if placement space runs out, also expand `halfWidth`, `rows`, or spacing. `corridorOffsets` provides the baseline horizontal route at each building band; randomization mirrors, reverses, shifts, scales and perturbs this pattern. Houses never overwrite roads or each other, so a very large requested count can be limited by available space.
- Different towns: `village.randomization` controls bend/width variation, row spacing, house counts and jitter, courtyard gaps, plaza count, and lift-station offsets. `enabled: true` and `seed: 0` generate fresh layouts each time a village is created; layouts stay fixed while you ski through them. A positive `seed` makes the layout sequence reproducible from the summit (resident activity remains independent). Set `enabled: false` for the original planned arrangement. Changes to row spacing automatically shift the town exit, lift, and course-choice row together.
- Town road surfaces: `village.mainRoadSurface` controls the main street and lift approach (`snow` by default, or `paved`). `snowRoadColors` contains the bank, packed-snow, highlight and track colors. Snow takes precedence over paving where routes cross.
- Longer village: expand its row layout and move `stationY`, `roadEndY`, `choiceTitleY`, `choiceY`, and `endOffset` down together. Keep the choice row below the houses and the village end below the choices.
- Ski-patrol punishment: `chasers.ambushCount.patrol` is 4. `chasers.patrolWaves.durationSeconds` is 120 and `intervalSeconds` is 5. The same section controls overtaking speed, lateral steering, lane spacing, pass lifetime and speech timing. `dialogue.patrolTaunts` contains the unpaid-ticket heckles. Course exits and finishes do not cancel waves.
- Fewer wolf encounters: lower `world.wolfShareOfPredators`, increase `wildlife.packSpawnSeparation`, or reduce `wildlife.packLimit`. Packs retain their configured 3–4 members.
- Fewer rabbits: remove rabbit entries from `spawnTables.human` / `wild` or add more copies of other objects.
- Preserve comparable best times after a substantial change: increment `timing.recordVersion`. Wallets remain intact.

Saved data uses the browser's `frostline-v2` local-storage key. `times[courseId]` contains the personal best, last attempt, and bounded attempt history; each history item has time, gate or mushroom results, crashes, payment status, speed bonus, date, and rules signature. Failed/unpaid attempts are recorded but never become qualifying best times.

Local-file and hosted versions have separate browser storage origins. Publishing does not move the offline wallet or records to the hosted site.



Arcade starts: `startStage` is 1-based; `autoStart: true` loads directly above that stage’s start. Starts above 1 are practice and do not overwrite records. start-line yetis and penalty wolves resolve from the stage's `knobs` via `arcade.knobLevels` (counts, chase speed as percent of top, guarded-gate share); no other automatic speed calculation applies.

New Arcade item keys: `fallingTree`, `logJump`, `rollingSnowball` (all `slightlyRare`). Set a per-stage `itemRates` value to control each, including `0` to disable. `terrainFeatures.logJump.widthMetres` is 100 (20× the initial version), `bumpWidthMetres` is independently 15 (3×), and `heightMetres` controls visual thickness; the bump is spawned with the log and is not a separate rate. Logs always center on the gate route at a between-gate midpoint, with the bump sharing their X coordinate. Blocked log placements retry neighbouring gaps without lateral scattering; existing opening, feature, landing and footprint clearances can still reject attempts. The old `snowball` stays stationary.

Rolling snowballs use `triggerDistanceMetres` as an arming range and time their warning/release from the skier's current velocity. They wait for a downhill skier and keep a fixed path after release. `downhillSpeedKmh` is the terminal downhill-speed baseline; the downward speed can adjust once at release to the projected crossing, capped at 125% of that setting. `lateralSpeedKmh` caps the side-to-side speed.

Falling pines rotate sideways with an animated collision capsule; `fallenRadiusMetres` controls its radius. Jumping clears the log, rotating/fallen pine, and rolling snowball. Snowballs have a 240 m arming range and 270 m travel limit. Their terminal downhill speed, acceleration time, initial speed fraction, sinusoidal sway amplitude/period, and trail lifetime are separately editable under `terrainFeatures.rollingSnowball`. Solid scenery causes a fading breakup animation; the trail persists afterward. See `ARCADE.md` for the release timing and speed cap.

Bear pursuit uses explicit world-unit tuning in `wildlife`: `bearChaseSpeed: 150` is 30 km/h downhill with the default HUD conversion; `bearChaseDistance: 10000` gives wild bears 1000 m of skier downhill travel before expiry. `bearWakeDistance: 340` is a 34 m activation radius, and `bearRoamSpeed: 40` is an 8 km/h four-legged walk. Course-owned bears still pursue until their run ends. The legacy `chasers.speedRatios.bear` field is retained for config compatibility but no longer controls bear helper spawns; all bear chases use `wildlife.bearChaseSpeed`. Course exits/off-piste notices and dog/patrol bumps do not shorten unrelated bear chases. Existing town, safe-approach and protected-piste rules remain active.

Enemy pursuit uses `predators.offDownhillSpeedRatio: 0.5`: horizontal X velocity and uphill Y velocity are halved, while downhill Y stays at its configured value. Applies to bears, yetis, hunting wolves, pursuing dogs and ski patrol, including avoidance turns. At a diagonal, X is halved independently of downhill Y. Ordinary wandering animals, friendly skiers and player physics keep their existing movement.

NPC prey selection checks `predators.divertChance` every `divertRecheckSeconds` (1.5 s) only while a yeti, bear or wolf has no committed NPC target. A selected target stays locked; the 8 s `divertTimeoutSeconds` now measures lack of progress rather than total hunt duration. Closing the best gap by at least one world unit (0.1 m) refreshes it. Eaten/dead targets are released immediately. Prey must initially be within 35 m and nearer the player than the hunter. This is larger than the 31 m skier fear radius so a hunter can acquire a target before it flees. Town/protected-area prey is excluded except for an active course-owned hunter on its own course. Foxes, wolves, bears and yetis are excluded as prey.

Yetis and bears additionally restrict prey to their direct route toward the player's current position. These optional `predators` fields have loader defaults for older configs:

| Setting | Default | Effect |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `divertPathWidthMetres` | 8 | Maximum sideways prey offset when selecting a target. |
| `divertPathAngleDegrees` | 30 | Maximum prey heading deviation from the direction to the player when selecting. |
| `divertReleaseWidthMetres` | 12 | Active hunt's maximum prey offset from the current route, and maximum hunter/prey offset from the diversion's starting point-to-current-player route. |
| `divertReleaseAngleDegrees` | 45 | Active hunt's maximum prey heading deviation before returning to the player. |

Prey must remain ahead of the hunter, before the player and nearer the player than the hunter. Wider release limits give small detours room without repeated target switching. The saved diversion origin prevents gradual sideways drift from redefining the route. A route-limit release resets old avoidance turns, resumes player pursuit that frame, and waits one `divertRecheckSeconds` interval before another selection attempt. Release width/angle must be at least their selection equivalents. Wolves retain their existing roaming hunts and do not use these limits.

Skier/snowboarder fear uses `wildlife.npcFearDistance` (310 world units / 31 m), `npcFearSeconds`, `npcFleeSpeed`, `npcFleeLateralSpeed` and `npcSpeechCooldown` for wolves, bears and awake Yetis. The nearest eligible enemy drives fleeing; towns/protected pistes suppress ordinary threats, while course-owned pursuers can frighten NPCs on their own piste. Dead, stunned, retiring, sleeping and waiting enemies are excluded. `dialogue.terrified` contains wolf warnings; `bearTerrified` and `yetiTerrified` supply the other warnings.

Falling-tree and rolling-ball `pathPlacementChance` defaults to 0.9: most placement attempts use the route between gate openings and a midpoint between gates. Their `pathOffsetMetres` pairs default to [4, 9] and [12, 24] respectively. Tree offsets are capped at 75% of height and nearby trees fall across the route; ball offsets are capped at 1.5× sway amplitude. Other ball placements also stay within the first bend's reach, with radius/player clearance deducted. Existing per-stage item rates are retained. See `ARCADE.md` for clearance rules. The temporary level-one log/tree/ball placements have been removed.

Casual skier turns use `skiers.slowTurnSeconds: [2.5, 5.5]`, `slowTurnChance: 0.8` (otherwise a straight stretch), `slowTurnLateralRatio: 0.5`, `slowSteeringFollow: 1.4`, and `slowTurnMaxOffsetMetres: 18`. Steering eases toward the chosen direction and turns back near the lane boundary, with a small overshoot. Downhill speed is retained. Fear resets the lane around the skier's escape position. Fast skier targeting uses actual distance `fastAimDistance: 350` (35 m), from ahead or behind; `fastAimWeight: 0.75` and `fastSteeringFollow: 1.2` produce gradual lane cutting, with the existing lateral cap and lead time. Outside range they ease toward straight downhill motion.


Special Arcade stages live in the same ordered `arcade.stages` map: Keep Your Momentum (9), Rush Hour (4), Aim Before Ice (6), Timber! (10), Snowball Run (12). Their matching `stageOverrides` use optional `specialLayout` (`none`, `yetiIntro`, `jumpLine`, `momentum`, `iceIslands`, `iceChaos`, `snowballs`, `timber`) for reusable arranged encounters, plus `briefing` for the start instruction. Layout encounters are independent of random `itemRates`; those rates add surrounding objects. `jumpLine` remains available for custom straight-gate stages; `momentum` keeps alternating slalom gates, varies large-hill/rainbow placement, and offers occasional jumps away from the next gate. Both reserve approach/landing space. Move stage rows to change insertion points; rename the override key with the stage if renaming. `startStage` remains a 1-based row number.

Arcade now links stages into groups of three. `arcade.stageFlow.townEveryStages: 3` puts towns after stage numbers 3, 6, 9, etc.; set 1 for the old every-stage cadence. Optional `arcade.stageOverrides["Stage name"].townAfter` forces (`true`) or skips (`false`) that stage's town boundary. Omission uses the global numbered cadence. For example, `Monster: {townAfter:false}` and `Rush Hour: {townAfter:true}` join stages 1–4, with the next default town still at 6. Merge these fields into existing overrides. A cadence larger than the stage count lets named `townAfter:true` overrides define all intermediate breaks. The final stage always ends the campaign.

`arcade.stageFlow.linkedStageGapMetres: 0` adds no extra gap: two half-spacing run-outs keep gate rhythm at joins. Increase it for a longer snow connector. These fields are optional with loader defaults of 3/0. Connected slopes are prepared in advance and retain surviving monsters, player speed/heading/jumps, combo and gear through internal boundaries. Per-stage gate counts and monster introductions still apply. Finish flags, spectators, result panels and protected towns appear at group ends; a town retires the entire group's predators. Practice starts proceed from the selected stage to its next town. Campaign version is 8 to archive records from the previous stage order. See ARCADE.md for an editable JSON example.

Optional per-stage `yetiSpeedPercent` overrides the chase knob’s Yeti speed percentage. `yetiSpeedKmh` sets an exact HUD speed and takes precedence over the percentage when both are present. Keep Your Momentum uses the user-configured exact 92 km/h with player speed knob 1 (90 km/h). Guard wolves retain the chase knob speed. Optional `yetiWakeSeconds` overrides the global 0.8 s start-line delay; Momentum uses 4 s, applying to its new Yetis while previous linked-stage hunters continue. `yetiColor: "magenta"` maps to the distinct `special` body tint/accent in `arcade.yetiTints`/`yetiColors`. These new fields/tints have loader defaults for older configs. The campaign revision is 8 after removing Air Time and Darker Woods, Icy Lakes. See ARCADE for the layouts and stage list.

Stage 2, Denser Forest, now uses chase knob 2 for one slow teal Yeti at 45 km/h and `specialLayout: "yetiIntro"` to keep its first three gate approaches clear. Its rare-object rate, dogs and extra pedestrians are zero so the first chase has fewer distractions. Before their focused stages, `itemRates` explicitly disable `logJump` (before Dense Chase, now stage 11), `fallingTree` (before Timber!), and `rollingSnowball` (before Snowball Run). These are editable stage settings, not a global unlock system.

Keep Your Momentum remains stage 9 after Fast Monster and 2 Fast 2 Forest. The current user tuning is 40 gates, a 90 km/h player top, three magenta Yetis at 92 km/h with a four-second wake delay, and a replay startStage 1 / autoStart false. Jump gaps vary by two to four gates, jump types are shuffled, and takeoff positions vary. Four interior jumps sit late on the previous gate's outer side, competing with the imminent opposite-side gate: take the gate for combo/gear or the jump for escape speed, then rejoin at the following gate. The other nine jumps can be aimed through their next gate. Landing limits leave steering room before the later slalom turn. Stage length remains about 2,698 m in practice; path interpolation follows actual gate positions. Trees and traffic respect the reserved approaches/landings. Rush Hour (4) now has 40 gates, about 1,365 m. Its optional Yeti-speed override is 70% (63 km/h), with zero start-line Yetis. The older iceIslands pattern remains available for custom practice stages. Keep Your Momentum (9) uses chase knob 4 for three Yetis, with large-hill/rainbow choices along its slalom route.

Village additions: village.names is a non-repeating name list until exhausted; village.backRoute has enabled, width (100 world units) and offset (310 world units) for a natural snow-only route behind houses. ## Endless Mixed Slalom and NPC crashes

`endless` is independent of `arcade`; existing Arcade rows and settings are unchanged. The summit puts all five entrances on the same row, in race.entryChoices order (Endless is first, on the left). Summit spacing and openings compact to fit the screen; ordinary village entrances keep their configured dimensions. `endless.autoStart` defaults to false; set true to start directly above the Endless line. If both mode auto-start flags are true, Endless takes priority. `recordVersion` controls its separate best-distance record. There is no finite course length or town cadence in this mode; `courses.endless` supplies the entry's name/colour, while its gameplay speed and gate spacing come from `endless`.

`playerSpeedKmh` is 90 and `maxPlayerSpeedKmh` is 130. Speed follows `start + (max - start) × (1 - exp(-distance / speedRampMetres))`, with a 2,400 m speed ramp: about 103 km/h at 1 km, 115 at 2.4 km and 125 at 5 km. Pace uses furthest distance, so walking uphill does not reverse progression. Difficulty has its own faster `difficultyRampMetres` (1,200, previously 2,800) and `waveMetres` (650) for changing forest/lake emphasis and brief relief.

The live speed multiplier is current top / starting top. Gate spacing/opening/swing, random lake dimensions/offsets, corridor geometry, generation lookahead/batches and Yeti encounter spacing and the local spawn-count radius scale by it. Owned traffic cruise/flee/steering and owned Yeti speeds also scale, including existing hunters. Ice acceleration/top speed scale for the player and owned NPCs. Wake/fear distances scale where applicable. Terrain attempt rates divide by the square of the local multiplier while the spread expands, preserving reaction time as speed rises instead of inadvertently doubling density. Actors keep their original sprite sizes. These changes apply to Endless; global Arcade/regular-course physics and tuning are unchanged.

The pairs in `rates` are initial/late rates for trees, skiers, fastSkiers, lakes and jumps. Trees fan out into pine/fir/spruce/small-pine rates; jumps include small hills and occasional large hills/rainbows. Normal placement clearances/spread scaling apply. Random lakes use a separate schedule with at least 110 base metres between centres, irregular existing shapes and a snowy bypass. First lake is at `firstLakeMetres` 220; radius varies within `lakeRadiusMetres` 20–34 before speed scaling. Random terrain and lakes stay out of the authored encounters.

`gates.spacingMetres` is 38 before scaling, `openingMetres` narrows 19→14 and `swingMetres` grows 11→16 each side. Every eighth gate is a sword gate; missing one resets combo without ending the run. Equipment still uses Arcade charge/cap settings. Inside encounters gates follow the available routes. At forks/junctions a wider shared opening splits into paired alternatives with one gate number: either route counts once, and the unused branch does not report a miss or award twice.

`encounters.firstMetres` is 450. `gapMetres` 300–480 and `lengthMetres` 900–1,200 are multiplied by local speed scale. Special sections are five times their former length. Every shuffled group of five includes town, forest, glacier, castle and mountain themes; adjacent groups cannot repeat a theme at the boundary. The first section remains a town. Each long route makes several broad excursions and offers rejoining detours, ranging roughly 130–210 m across in typical generated examples, rather than following the old narrow centre strip. Gates follow the routes; either branch counts once. `routeSwingMetres` 38–55 controls the base excursion. Only approaching rows and curved street segments are populated in the usual internal batches, so the full kilometre is not created at once. Endless road tiles use direct segment rasterization, avoiding the old full-route oversampling cost. Ambient terrain is excluded across the authored section, including its distant detours.

Each theme counts its own appearances. `difficultyVisits` 6 reaches the final layout difficulty on that theme's sixth appearance, with five equal steps between. Its difficulty is frozen at planning time; individual rows do not get harder partway through a section. Global Endless speed still rises with distance. `corridorWidthMetres` now supplies the late width (14.4) and first-visit width (23); forest/town/castle/mountain lanes taper 23→14.4 base metres, while glacier snow lanes taper only 23→20.16. These widths receive the section's speed scale. Towns and castles now have a single gate pair only at each shared route rejoin (roughly five checkpoints per section), so side routes stay free of duplicate flags. These checkpoints alternate shield/sword rewards, weighted for their wider spacing to keep approximate equipment supply per metre at full combo. Inside a special section, gate openings use that section's frozen difficulty/scale and are clamped to their local passage; their opening and rhythm do not tighten row by row.

- Towns reuse regular house/shop facades, hotels, animated residents and handouts, lamps and playful snow props. The main snow street has natural snow-only back lanes between the houses, splitting and rejoining repeatedly. Each street row has 1→5 crossing residents as later towns get narrower; first-town crossing crowds are halved, and the chance of a resident by a building starts at 16% rather than 32%, rising to 32% in later towns. Resident/building sprites retain the existing small scale.
- Forests gain denser tree walls (row spacing 7.5→5.4 base metres), deeper banks of trees (14→21 m), narrower passages and five→six sets of turns with shorter turn-in distances.
- Glaciers have broad irregular frozen lakes, snowy steering islands and a longer dry-bank detour. Later glaciers have 3→1 islands per lake and longer uninterrupted ice (48→68% of each route cycle). Hunters choose the dry route. Cached pixel ice art adds depth bands, fracture fans, frosted banks and blue cliff faces, with snowy mountain ranges outside.
- Castle ruins alternate moderate courtyards (up to 1.8× breach width) and narrow wall breaches. Later visits shrink the breaches; low rubble and a nearby bump offer optional jumping lines. Standing walls and mountain/glacier cliffs block jumps; low rubble remains jumpable. Weathered masonry, compact keeps with windows/buttresses, snow caps, small broken gateway pillars and buried paving distinguish the ruins.
- Mountain passes (`High Pass`, `Snowcap Traverse`, `Split Summits`) use snowy switchbacks between tall, connected pixel crags with bright caps and shaded rock faces. Later passes narrow and increase from four to six sets of turns with sharper entries.

Course towns have no finish, lift, stage result, course-entry flags or gear reset. Only hunters owned by the active Endless run may pursue into these towns; they can attack there and the player can use gear. Ambient spawning is blocked and Endless Yeti placements reject town interiors. Regular villages retain their protection. Encounter pursuit uses a committed branch waypoint so predators follow passages and rejoin beyond the obstacle instead of steering into the separating wall.

`yetis.firstMetres` is 420. Nearby capacity grows one per `additionalEveryMetres` 650 up to `maxNearbyHunters` 4, counting dormant placements and awake hunters no farther behind than `nearbyMetres` 180 (speed-scaled). `intervalMetres` tapers 450→240 base metres. Each hunter receives a 68–94 km/h base from progression; the common live speed multiplier then applies to all owned hunters (late maximum about 135.8 km/h versus player 130). Faster base tiers use orange/magenta. They wake with 2.5 s notice, avoid ice, and hunt NPCs on the way to the player. Awake hunters never retire for distance. Only dormant placements are removed behind that radius; distant awake enemies keep pursuing at their real speed and do not occupy the nearby spawn budget, allowing new threats while older enemies accumulate. Bears, wild Yetis and alerted wolves encountered during Endless join this persistent pursuit as well. Wolves retain their linked movement and survive scenery pruning. The old `maxActive`/`retireBehindMetres` names remain loader aliases for older configs. There is no teleportation or extra distance-based catch-up acceleration. Defeating an enemy or ending the run still ends its pursuit.

`sectionMetres` 240 and `lookAheadMetres` 180 are base generation sizes before speed scaling. Batches are internal and never stop the player. Retained batch/encounter/road/town data and far-ahead fast traffic stay bounded. Best distance saves every 250 m and on death/reset. `recordVersion` is now 3 for the longer sections; the old Endless record is retained under `previousVersion`. Arcade records and wallets remain separate.

`skiers.crashSeconds: [4, 5]` sets the NPC down time, including the final `getUpSeconds` (0.6) animation. Skier/boarder collisions use swept movement and actual obstacle footprints, with a nearby-object grid. Trees, rocks, trunks, houses, sleds, moving snowballs and other skiers can knock them down; flags, ice and helpful hills do not. Downed NPCs stop moving and remain edible. On recovery they steer away from the previous collision and resume skiing. Player/NPC impacts can knock both down. Killing a predator clears an NPC's fear but does not instantly heal their fall. Player falls clear the crash-created shield; obstacle contacts remain suppressed while down, but Yetis/bears/wolves can catch the player during recovery. Equipment protection and brief resume protection still work.
